## sail 0.25.0 (pre-release)

### Breaking changes

- **`quic.window_budget`, `quic.free_memory_floor` and
  `quic.anon_memory_max` are now `memory.transfer_budget`,
  `memory.free_memory_floor` and `memory.anon_memory_max`**: the budget
  covers accepted connections' send buffers as well as the QUIC windows.
  The old keys are not read.
- **A connection's closed record is never lost, and interim records
  carry totals** (sail#24): with `Options::connection_records_lossless`,
  every closed record is kept for the reader. Records past the cap are
  no longer folded into summaries (`Record::Folded` and `RecordSummary`
  are gone, and `RecordStream::next` gives the record itself). While
  `cap` closed records wait for the reader, new connections are refused,
  counted by `RecordStream::refused`, until it takes one. Records carry
  the bytes since the connection started, `up_total` and `down_total`;
  `up`, `down` and `since` are gone, from the event, its JSON (`since_ms`)
  and the bindings. Interim records of a timed pass are dropped while the
  queue is full, counted by `RecordStream::dropped`.
  `Instance::report_connections` (`POST /connections/report`) now
  records every open connection, idle ones too, and a lossless sink keeps
  all of them.

### Embedding

- **A closed record tells how the connection was used** (sail#24):
  `outcome` is `Ok`, `DialFailed`, `Blocked` or `Rejected`. A connection
  that never relayed now has a closed record too: a dial that failed, a
  reject or drop rule, a user refused at its limits. Records also carry
  `local`, the address the client connected to, and `outbound`, the tag
  the rules chose.

- **Limits a host sets for a user stay through a reload** (sail#26):
  `set_user_limits` and the management API's `PUT /users/{name}/limits`
  set an override over `user_limits`, which a reload keeps unless it
  changes what the configuration sets for that user. Then the override is
  dropped, and `ReloadReport::user_limits_dropped` (`user_limits_dropped`
  in its JSON) names the user. `restore_user_limits` drops it. A reload
  used to put back the configuration's limits for every user.

### Configuration

- **`user_limits.<name>.total_mbps`, one rate up and down together**
  (sail#26): both directions draw from it, by every connection of the
  user, alongside `up_mbps` and `down_mbps` where those are set. It
  changes live through `set_user_limits`, like the others.

### Fixed

- **`inbound.tcp_send_buffer` is the size asked for** (sail#10): Linux
  stops a plain request at `net.core.wmem_max`, 208 KiB by default, so
  the router profile's 256 KiB was 208 on most kernels. sail now asks past
  the limit, as it does for `inbound.tcp_receive_buffer`, where it has
  CAP_NET_ADMIN, as on a router.
- **Shrinking a TCP send buffer keeps allowance for data still queued**
  (sail#10): lowering `SO_SNDBUF` does not release that memory. Its
  excess allowance now stays in `memory.transfer_budget` until a write
  observes drainage, or the connection closes; if the queue cannot be
  queried, it stays reserved until close. Growth reads back the actual
  size and refunds only unused new allowance. Failed readback keeps the
  requested growth charged. An idle or canceled writer may retain
  drained allowance until its next write or close. Close returns Sail's
  reservation, not necessarily all of TCP's queued memory.
- **An idle connection takes what it took before 0.24.0:** in 0.24.0
  each routed connection's number kept room for a copy of its session,
  about a kilobyte, which only a session told to a host needs. On a
  server an idle Shadowsocks 2022 connection took 1.16 times what it took
  before and a Trojan one 1.09 times; now 0.95 and 1.01.
- **Clients that stop reading no longer stall the other streams on
  their Hysteria2 or TUIC connection** (`quic.credit_on_receipt`,
  sail#18): a connection gave its peer credit back only as stream data
  was read, so two or three streams nobody read, such as paused
  downloads, used up its window and left the others none. sing-box
  (quic-go) does the same. The credit is now given as data arrives,
  lent out of `memory.transfer_budget` until the stream reads it, so a
  stalled stream holds its own window and no more; where the budget is
  spent, credit comes back on read as before. A stream left unread for
  `mux.stall_timeout` (60 s), which QUIC streams now take as the
  multiplexers do, is reset, and its loan repaid. On a router, 4 LAN
  downloads beside 20 clients that stopped reading went from nothing to
  2.4–3.7 Gbit/s; sing-box's still get nothing (measured, sail#18).
- **A TUIC client logs why its connection closed** (at debug, the
  application's code or the transport error and its frame), as a
  Hysteria2 client already did; it dropped a closed connection silently.
- **A yamux or amux stream dropped before the peer finished it is reset
  alone** (sail#21): its session had taken no new streams since, and
  closed after its last, so that every cancelled request over a
  long-lived yamux connection between two nodes cost a new connection.
  What the peer sent the stream before it heard is now dropped as it
  comes, and the session goes on. smux and AnyTLS, which cannot reset one
  stream, retire their session as before. A stream dropped before the
  peer's FIN is reset, where Go's yamux `Close` sends a FIN.
- **A yamux client waits while 256 of its streams are unacknowledged**
  (sail#22), as hashicorp/yamux's client and rust-yamux do: it opened
  every stream at once, and hashicorp/yamux resets those past its accept
  backlog of 256, so that of 500 streams opened at once some failed. All
  500 now complete against it, which CI checks on every change, both
  ways (tested).

### Default changes

- **A router's QUIC endpoint takes 8 datagrams per receive, not 32**
  (`quic.udp_receive_batch`, sail#10): the room it keeps for them falls
  from about 3 MB to 0.75. In a 64 MiB cgroup downloads peaked 2–3 MiB
  lower and no cell was slower, on the LAN at 0 and 8 ms and at 150 and
  300 ms. Zero keeps quinn's 32, as other profiles do.
- **A router's LAN downloads grow their send buffer out of one memory
  budget** (`inbound.tcp_send_buffer_max`, sail#10): an accepted TCP
  connection starts at `inbound.tcp_send_buffer` (256 KiB) and doubles,
  up to 2 MiB, while that buffer rather than the network or the client
  holds the download back, out of the budget Hysteria2's and TUIC's
  windows grow from. While writes are polled, the configured size returns
  to its starting value after a second without filling the buffer, or a
  second with a blocked write; queued excess keeps its budget allowance
  as described above. In earlier measurements, four downloads by a
  client 8 ms away on a 4 Gbit/s link, in a 64 MiB cgroup, went from 0.34
  of sing-box's to 0.88-0.91, peaking at 37.8 MiB;
  with 20 clients that stopped reading beside them the peak was 42.2 MiB
  (measured, sail#10). These measurements preceded the queue-accounting
  and readback fixes above. Other profiles leave the buffer to the
  system as before.
