## sail 0.20.0 (pre-release)

A minor release on top of 0.19.2. It changes some defaults (below), reads
more of sing-box's, Mihomo's and Surge's configuration, and takes less
memory on phones and routers. The C ABI and the Swift and Kotlin APIs
are as in 0.19.2, with two calls added (sleep and wake). It includes the
fixes of 0.19.1 and 0.19.2.

Documentation: https://peakpassvpn.github.io/sail/

### Default changes

- **The mobile profile keeps at most 512 sessions at once,** as the router
  profile does: one more waits up to a second for a place and is then
  refused. `--set inbound.max_connections=0` lifts it.
- **Fewer worker threads on desktops and phones.** The desktop profile
  runs two workers (one on a single CPU), the mobile profile one, with the
  CLI as embedded; server and router keep one per CPU. `--threads` and
  `Options::threads` still win.
- **QUIC sockets ask for larger receive buffers:** 7 MiB on the desktop
  and server profiles, 1 MiB on mobile and router
  (`quic.udp_receive_buffer`; 0 keeps the system's). On Linux with
  `CAP_NET_ADMIN` the size goes past `net.core.rmem_max`.
- **An inbound's UDP socket sets don't-fragment** unless its new
  `udp_fragment` is true, as sing-box's does; direct and Hysteria2
  inbounds fragment by default, as in sing-box.
- **The log writer queues 4,096 lines,** not 128,000. Lines logged faster
  than they are written are dropped, as before, and now counted: the count
  is logged at the next reload or at stop.
- **A wake from sleep no longer drops connections.** A wake is no change
  of network: after a sleep shorter than 2 minutes nothing is closed;
  after a longer one only the carriers left idle are (pooled connections
  with nothing open, the DNS servers' among them), and the groups test
  their members again, as sing-box 1.14.3 does. Connections in use stay.
  Before, any sleep over 5 seconds closed every connection.
- **Idle relay buffers kept for reuse** are bounded by the new
  `relay.buffer_pool`: 256 KiB a thread on the mobile and router profiles,
  1 MiB elsewhere as before.
- **An outbound's idle connections close after 60 s with nothing open:**
  Hysteria2's, the QUIC transport's, gRPC's and amux's, as mux's sessions
  already did, the member a group selects included (sing-box keeps that
  one). The next connection dials anew.

### New

- **`ntp`** (sing-box's): the time the protocols check, certificate
  validity among them, is corrected by an NTP server's, never the
  system's clock. For routers that start without a clock.
- **sing-box fields read:** an outbound's `network` (`tcp` or `udp`); the
  TUN's `dns_mode` and `udp_filtering: endpoint_independent` (full cone,
  also as `endpoint_independent_nat`); an inbound's `tcp_fast_open` and
  `udp_fragment`; sing-box 1.15's `tls.certificate_sha256` in base64,
  together with the public-key pins; an HTTP outbound's and HTTP client's
  `version`, `engine` and `disable_version_fallback`, checked as sing-box
  checks them (sail speaks HTTP/1.1 either way, and warns).
- **Groups:** a load-balance group tests its members as url-test does
  (`timeout`, `max_failed_times`, `expected_status`); any group may time
  its members as Mihomo's `unified-delay`; load-balance `hash_key: "user"`
  keys on the inbound's user.
- **Dial and DNS:** `tcp_concurrent` dials every address at once; an
  https DNS server with `prefer_h3` asks over HTTP/3 beside HTTP/2.
- **The host's sleep and wake:** `Instance::sleep()` and `wake()` in the
  embedding, `sail_sleep` and `sail_wake` in the C ABI, `sleep()` and
  `wake()` in Swift and Kotlin, as libbox's `Pause` and `Wake` (iOS: the
  extension's `sleep` and `wake`). Timed checks and updates wait while
  asleep; the sleep is timed between the two calls.
- **A smart group's sites are kept across restarts** with
  `experimental.cache_file`: each site's member and how the members did
  for it, the names hashed with a salt of the file's own. Sites learnt on
  another network, or older than `site_ttl`, are dropped when read back.
- **Inbounds:** `skip_auth_prefixes` lets clients from given prefixes in
  without credentials (http, socks, mixed).
- **Inbound TLS checks clients' certificates** as sing-box's
  `client_authentication`, `client_certificate`, `client_certificate_path`
  and `client_certificate_public_key_sha256` (and 1.15's
  `client_certificate_sha256`) say, over TCP and QUIC (Hysteria2, TUIC,
  the `quic` transport). A changed CA file, or a reload that edits these
  fields, is taken without rebinding the inbound. Settings that would
  leave clients unchecked while looking checked are errors.
- REALITY inbounds may accept several server names
  (`tls.reality.server_names`), as Xray's and Mihomo's servers do.
- **Clash/Mihomo profiles:** `tcp-concurrent`, `tfo` and `inbound-tfo`,
  `keep-alive-idle`, `keep-alive-interval` and `disable-keep-alive`,
  `skip-auth-prefixes`, `global-ua`, `geo-auto-update` and
  `geo-update-interval`, `unified-delay`, a load-balance group's `lazy`,
  `timeout`, `max-failed-times`, `expected-status` and `hash-key: in-user`,
  every group's `disable-udp`, and `tun.endpoint-independent-nat`.
- **Clash/Mihomo listeners:** an http listener's `certificate` and
  `private-key` are its TLS; trojan listeners are trojan inbounds, with
  their TLS (or `allow-insecure`), a websocket (`ws-path`) or gRPC
  (`grpc-service-name`), and sing-mux, which they serve as Mihomo's do
  (`mux-option`); vless listeners likewise, with their users' `flow`; and
  `reality-config` is REALITY on http, trojan and vless listeners, whose
  `client-auth-type` and `client-auth-cert` check clients' certificates
  as Mihomo's do.
- **Surge profiles:** `block-quic = all` refuses every QUIC connection.
- **HTTPUpgrade:** a server answers a `Sec-WebSocket-Key` with its
  `Sec-WebSocket-Accept`, as Xray's does.

### Fixed

- A CIDR with bits set past its prefix (`10.1.2.3/8`) stands for its
  network, as in sing-box; such configurations were refused.
- A `--set` keeps the profile it was given with: after one, sail took
  the instance for a desktop wherever the profile decides something.
- A fake-IP range written with bits set past its prefix
  (`inet4_range: 28.0.0.1/8`) starts at the address written and ends at
  its network's last, as sing-box takes it; it was refused.
- A reload keeps the rule-sets that did not change instead of reading them
  again, and no longer keeps the start's rule-sets alive after the first
  reload.
- A reload that changes the NetFilter inbound (Windows) keeps its driver
  and its fake addresses.
- The fallback, REALITY and ShadowTLS relays to the real site have the
  relay's write stall and idle timeouts; a site that stopped reading held
  the connection for good.
- A multiplexed session (smux, AnyTLS) whose connection takes nothing ends
  after the write stall timeout instead of hanging.
- gRPC: a server pings only after its idle time with nothing received, and
  bytes from the peer keep a connection whose pong is late.
- A group member that carries one network is neither tested for the other
  nor sent it (url-test, fallback, load-balance, smart); a load-balance
  sticky session is kept per network.
- VLESS Vision clients check the response's version and skip its addons.
- Hysteria2 and TUIC bound what unfinished UDP packets may hold; AnyTLS
  takes control frames as the protocol defines them and bounds a pushed
  padding scheme.
- The TUN stack counts an ACK at a shut or full window, and a shut peer
  window no longer times a flow out.

### Memory and performance

- **Less memory for many rules.** A compiled rule takes about half what
  it did; a Clash or Surge profile's rules merge as they are read, so
  reading one no longer peaks above the running instance. With 30,000
  rules on a router build: at rest 46 to 29 MiB (sing-box format) and 29
  to 23 MiB (Clash), a reload's peak 86 to 67 MiB and 55 to 31 MiB.
- **Less memory a connection.** An idle connection keeps room for its
  relay, not for its dial and handshake: about 13 to 8 KB of heap for a
  Trojan connection over the TUN.
- **DNS cache:** a cached answer takes 2 to 4 times less memory, kept as
  the bytes it came as (an A answer about 300 bytes with its key, 700
  before; a CNAME chain 375, 1,500 before), and is read outside the
  cache's lock. With a Clash profile's 4,096 entries that is at most
  about 1.5 MB.
- **TUN with offload:** a flow's batch beyond one GSO frame goes out as
  several frames, not a segment at a time: a Trojan download over the TUN
  went from 0.89 to 1.23 of sing-box's (4856 to 6721 Mbit/s on the test
  host).
- **QUIC** sends up to 16 GSO segments at once.

### For plugin authors

- `ExternalOutboundStreamHandler::handle_first` passes the client's first
  data with the handshake, so a plugin can send it with its header. It
  has a default, but a plugin built outside the tree must be rebuilt
  against this sail.
