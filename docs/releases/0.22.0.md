## sail 0.22.0 (pre-release)

A minor release on top of 0.21.0. The host's network-changed call now
blocks until the change is handled (below); smart groups rank downloads
by throughput and race a member slow to connect; the TUN stack caps what
a connection holds for its client and no longer wedges when its memory
is used up; mux sessions take far less memory. Apart from
network-changed, the C ABI and the Swift and Kotlin APIs are as in
0.21.0.

Documentation: https://peakpassvpn.github.io/sail/

### Breaking changes

- **The host's network-changed call blocks, for up to 10 s: call it off
  the main thread.** `network_changed` in the C ABI (`sail_network_changed`),
  and the Swift and Kotlin `networkChanged` over it, returns once the
  change is handled, with the old network's connections and TUN flows
  closed, so a host that goes on to use the new network no longer races
  them. It returned at once before, and a host could call it on the main
  thread. On that thread it now risks an ANR on Android and a hang of the
  UI on iOS and macOS: call it on a thread or queue that may block. After
  10 s it returns anyway and logs a warning. The Rust embed API's
  `network_changed` future likewise resolves once the change is handled.

### Default changes

- **A TUN connection holds at most `netstack.send_buffer` for its client**
  (new; 1024 KiB on the desktop and server profiles, 256 KiB on mobile
  and router): sent and not yet acknowledged, or waiting to be sent. A
  download no longer holds up to the client's receive window in the
  stack's memory; download speed over TUN was unchanged (Trojan, 4
  streams: 128 KiB was the first value that cost any, 15% on the server
  profile). `0` lifts the limit.

- **Smart groups rank a site that downloads by throughput, not latency:**
  once a connection to a site moved 1 MiB, the site's members are ranked
  by the bytes they moved for it over the time they took, and it tries a
  member that carried more elsewhere. A member that answers first but
  loses packets no longer keeps a site's downloads (at 2% loss on the
  quicker member: 98% of the faster member's throughput, against 13–100%
  before). Sites with short connections are ranked as before.
- **Smart groups race a member that is slow to connect, on a site's
  first connection:** when a site has no member yet and the first one
  tried is not ready within twice its usual connect time (250 ms while
  unknown), the next is dialled beside it and the first ready carries
  the connection; the other is closed and not blamed. A site that has a
  member is not raced, so it keeps its exit, but gives up on a stalled
  member after four of its connect times (1.5 s at least) instead of the
  group's `timeout`. Datagrams are tried in turn as before.
- **A smart group's member that no connection went through lately ranks
  by its latest probe:** it used to average its probes, so that one slow
  first probe, at a cold start, kept a faster member behind for several
  intervals; the first round of probes is also done again two seconds
  after it.
- **The OpenWrt package raises the kernel's TCP receive cap by default:**
  its `tcp_buffers` option, now on, sets the third value of the
  system-wide `net.ipv4.tcp_rmem` to 6 MiB, never lowering a larger one,
  so that one download over a TCP-based upstream is no longer held to the
  small cap the kernel sets by a router's memory (at 100 ms: 12–14 Mbit/s
  on 128 MiB and 34–37 on 256 MiB before, about 110 after). Memory under
  stalled connections was unchanged. `uci set sail.main.tcp_buffers=0`
  turns it off. sail itself is unchanged.

### Fixed

- **emSign ECC Root CA - G3 is no longer trusted** (RUSTSEC-2026-0383):
  intermediates under it whose private keys are known were distrusted,
  and the root is gone from the bundled Mozilla roots
  (webpki-root-certs 1.0.10), which stand in where the system has no
  store, and from the `mozilla` and `chrome` stores of `certificate`.
- **The TUN stack no longer wedges once its memory budget is used up**
  (peakpassvpn/sail#6). With `netstack.budget=mobile`, about 500
  connections stalled for good, and every new connection and DNS query
  failed until a restart:
  - the TCP bytes connections held for their readers (grown receive
    windows, bytes read ahead) counted towards the pressure that refuses
    every new flow, UDP included. They no longer do: a new TCP
    connection is refused only when its starting window does not fit.
  - those bytes could take the whole budget and leave none to send with.
    Starting windows now take at most three quarters of it, and grown
    windows and bytes read ahead at most half; sends have the rest.
  - a connection closed while the stack had no room for its reset or
    its close stayed in the stack, its memory held. It is let go of once
    there is room.

### Memory and performance

- **A mux session holds its 64 KiB read and write buffers only while it
  is busy:** AnyTLS, smux, yamux and amux sessions start reading into
  4 KiB, double it while reads fill it, and go back to 4 KiB after a
  second with nothing to read; the write batch is as large as what is
  queued, and is given back once nothing is. Each held 128 KiB for its
  whole life before, idle or pooled. With 500 AnyTLS connections, one
  stream to a session, open at both ends in one process: 157 MiB of heap
  before, 37 after; pooled after they closed, 38 MiB before, 8 after.
  Throughput was unchanged.
